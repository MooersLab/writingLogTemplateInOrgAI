* Overview
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: overview
:END:
#+Latex:\index{overview}
\index{AI agents!overview}
\index{manuscript assembly!protocol}

This document is an expanded form of the writing log template (version 0.8.5) that serves as a detailed protocol for manuscript assembly.
Each section of the original writing log has been augmented with three new subsections:

1. *AI Agent Dos*: Six to twenty specific, actionable ways in which an AI agent (such as Claude, GPT-4, Gemini, or a local LLM) can assist with the tasks in that section.
2. *AI Agent Do-Nots*: Ways in which AI agents are especially unreliable, inappropriate, or risky for that section. These are the guardrails.
3. *AI Verification Chores*: Concrete steps you can take---often with AI assistance---to verify that what the AI returned is correct.

The guiding principle is: /use AI as a tireless research assistant and first-draft generator, but never as the final authority on scientific claims, ethical judgments, or creative voice/.
Think of AI as a very fast but occasionally confused graduate student: you would not submit their first draft without checking it, and you would not trust them to choose which experiments to run.

This protocol draws on the book "Scientific Writing with Claude Cowork and Code" by Blaine Mooers (2026), which documents systematic workflows for AI-assisted academic writing at manuscript and book length. The book identifies four recurring themes that guide responsible AI use:

1. *Verification is not optional.* Every claim must be checked against sources. AI hallucinates with confidence, making verification a structural necessity, not an aspirational best practice.
2. *The human author remains the author.* The AI generates text; the human decides what to say and whether it is correct. Authorship is determined by judgment and responsibility, not by keystroke volume.
3. *Reproducibility extends to the writing process.* Just as you document experimental methods so others can replicate your work, you should document your AI-assisted writing process so others understand how the manuscript was created and can evaluate it appropriately.
4. *LaTeX compilation is a solved problem with iteration.* Multi-pass compilation, error correction loops, and integration with version control are well-established practices. AI can automate these tasks, freeing you for the creative and intellectual work of writing.

\index{power tool analogy}

Claude Code is a power tool for academic writing. Like a power saw or a 3D printer, it amplifies the author's productivity. But it also amplifies the consequences of unchecked assumptions. Use it to extend your reach and speed your work, but never to bypass your judgment.

\index{time savings!illusory}

A critical caution: AI compresses seven hours of initial drafting into twenty minutes. This is real, but it is only two to five percent of the total effort required to publish a high-quality manuscript. The hidden costs are verification (confirming claims), integration (ensuring consistency across sections), and revision (infusing your authorial voice and interpretive perspective). If you save two hours on drafting but then spend ten hours verifying and revising, the time savings are illusory.

\index{mental health!FOMO!nihilism}

Be aware also that working with AI can affect your mental health. Some experience FOMO (fear of missing out on faster peers) or nihilism (a sense that your work is fungible, replaceable, generic). These are real risks. Counteract them by maintaining your writing practice as a source of meaning and identity, not merely a means to publication.

\index{Adapt, do not adopt principle}

The core principle for responsible AI use in academic writing is: *Adapt, do not adopt.* Do not adopt AI workflows wholesale. Adapt them to your field, your writing voice, your standards for evidence, and your judgment about what matters. This protocol provides a framework, not a prescription.

** Claude Cowork versus Claude Code
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:cowork-vs-code
:END:
#+Latex:\index{Claude Cowork vs Code}
\index{AI agents!Claude Cowork}
\index{AI agents!Claude Code}

Claude Cowork and Claude Code are two interfaces to the same Claude models, but they serve different purposes. Understanding when to use each optimizes your workflow.

*** Claude Cowork
\index{AI agents!Claude Cowork!characteristics}

Claude Cowork is a browser-based interface optimized for conversation and collaboration. Best for:
- Initial exploration and idea generation
- Quick drafting of short sections
- Real-time discussion with the AI
- Sharing conversations with collaborators via links
- Working on non-file tasks (e.g., brainstorming, planning)

Limitations:
- Cannot reliably create or edit files in your local project
- Slower file operations
- Context limited to the browser session
- Less precise control over computational tasks

*** Claude Code
\index{AI agents!Claude Code!characteristics}

Claude Code is a terminal-based interface that operates directly on your file system. Best for:
- Batch file operations (processing multiple files)
- Integration with your version control (Git)
- Running scripts and shell commands
- Precise file editing and creation
- Long-running tasks (compiling, testing, analyzing)
- Reproducible workflows

Advantages:
- Direct file system access with permission gates
- Integration with LaTeX compilation and testing
- Session persistence across multiple commands
- Structured output suitable for scripting

*** Decision Tree
\index{AI agents!Cowork vs Code!decision}

Use Cowork when: Your task is primarily conversational, you are exploring ideas, or you need to share work in progress with human collaborators.

Use Code when: Your task involves file creation or modification, you are running scripts or compilation, or you need reproducible, verifiable workflows.

** The Model Context Protocol (MCP)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:mcp
:END:
#+Latex:\index{Model Context Protocol}
\index{MCP}
\index{AI agents!MCP}

MCP is a standardized protocol that allows language models to interact with external services and databases. It enables Claude to search academic databases, retrieve full-text articles, and verify claims against authoritative sources.

*** What MCP Solves
\index{AI agents!MCP!what it solves}

Without MCP, the AI can only use knowledge from its training data, which has a knowledge cutoff (Claude was trained on data through early 2024). MCP allows the AI to:
- Query PubMed for current biomedical literature
- Search ArXiv for preprints in physics, CS, and mathematics
- Look up DOIs and retrieve article metadata from CrossRef
- Search Semantic Scholar for papers by title or author

This solves the hallucination problem for recent publications and allows real-time verification of citations.

*** Available Academic MCP Servers
\index{AI agents!MCP!academic servers}

*PubMed MCP*: Search biomedical and life sciences literature. Retrieve article metadata, abstracts, MeSH terms, and links to PubMed Central for full-text articles. Rate limit: Respect NCBI quotas; use conservative query strategies.

*Semantic Scholar MCP*: Title-based search when DOI is unavailable. Author name search. Citation networks and related papers. No strict rate limits but respect server load.

*CrossRef MCP*: DOI resolution to retrieve article metadata. Publisher information. Citation validation. Very low rate limits; use judiciously.

*ArXiv MCP*: Preprint discovery for physics, CS, mathematics, and quantitative biology. Full-text availability for most papers. Categorization by subject area. Metadata includes authors and abstracts.

*** Ethical Access
\index{AI agents!MCP!ethical access}

When using MCP servers:
1. Respect rate limits. Too many queries overload servers; stagger requests.
2. Use specific queries rather than broad searches. Narrow queries return fewer results and consume fewer resources.
3. Cache results. If you query "CRISPR gene editing" once, do not query it again. Use the cached results.
4. Document your searches. Record the database, query, date, and number of results. This is part of reproducibility.

** Session Persistence and Context Management
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:session-persistence
:END:
#+Latex:\index{session persistence}
\index{context management!session persistence}

Claude Code maintains conversation state across interactions, allowing you to build up context across multiple sessions. This enables long-form projects (entire book chapters) that would exceed a single conversation's token budget.

*** Context Window Constraints
\index{AI agents!context window}

The AI's context window is the amount of text it can attend to simultaneously. Claude has a context window of approximately 200,000 tokens. For a typical manuscript:
- 10,000 tokens ≈ 7,500 words of prose
- Full context window ≈ 150,000 words of prose

A long chapter (5,000-8,000 words) uses about 30-40 percent of the context window. Accumulating drafts, grounding documents, and conversation quickly approach the limit. Strategic context management extends your effective session length.

*** "Story So Far" Pattern
\index{AI agents!Story So Far}

When context nears capacity, summarize prior work in a "Story So Far" document. This document contains:
- Project scope and goals
- Decisions made to date
- Current status of each section
- Next action

This summary uses far fewer tokens than including the full prior conversation, allowing the AI to continue with fresh context.

*** Progressive Disclosure
\index{AI agents!progressive disclosure}

Introduce information in phases rather than all at once. For a chapter with five sections:
- Session 1: Provide introduction materials. Draft introduction only.
- Session 2: Provide methods materials. Draft methods only. (You may reuse the story-so-far to restore context.)
- Session 3: Provide results. Draft results.
- Session 4: Provide literature. Draft discussion.
- Session 5: Integrate all sections, revise for coherence.

This approach keeps context focused and prevents information overload.

** Permission Model and Human-in-the-Loop Safety
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:permission-model
:END:
#+Latex:\index{permission model}
\index{human-in-the-loop!safety}

Claude Code implements a permission model where the AI must request approval before modifying your files. This gate is critical. You are responsible for every change to your project, and the AI cannot act unilaterally.

*** When Approval Is Required
\index{AI agents!permission model!approval}

Approval gates apply to:
- Creating new files
- Modifying existing files
- Deleting files
- Running shell commands that could have side effects
- Making external API calls (submitting work, publishing, etc.)

*** Your Responsibility
\index{AI agents!permission model!responsibility}

Every approval you grant is your decision. If you approve a command that has unintended consequences, you are accountable. Review what the AI proposes before approving. This is not paranoia; this is responsibility.

*** Implications
\index{AI agents!permission model!implications}

The permission model means AI-assisted workflows are inherently slower than fully automated workflows. This is by design. Slowness is safety.

** The Stochastic Trap: When to Automate vs. Script
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:stochastic-trap
:END:
#+Latex:\index{stochastic trap}
\index{LLM!non-deterministic}
\index{scripting!when to use}

LLMs are non-deterministic. The same prompt given twice produces different outputs. This creates a trap: if you rely on the AI for repetitive tasks, the AI may do some instances correctly and others incorrectly. You will miss the errors because you expect consistency.

*** The Problem
\index{AI agents!stochastic trap!problem}

Suppose you ask the AI to extract author names from 100 paper abstracts. The AI does this 95 times correctly and 5 times incorrectly. You might not notice the errors (expecting consistency), and the garbage data propagates downstream. By the time you discover the error, you have built analysis on corrupted data.

*** The Solution: Convert Repetitive Tasks to Scripts
\index{AI agents!stochastic trap!solution}

For any repetitive task:
1. Have the AI write a Python or Bash script to automate the task.
2. The script is deterministic (same input always produces same output).
3. You verify the script on a small test case.
4. Run the script on all your data.
5. Spot-check the output.

This trades the stochasticity of LLM output for the reliability of code.

*** Example
\index{AI agents!stochastic trap!example}

Instead of: "Extract the first author from each of these 100 paper citations."

Ask: "Write a Python script that reads a CSV file with a 'citation' column, extracts the first author name using regex, and outputs a new CSV with the first author in a new column. Test the script on this sample citation: [example]."

Then run the script once on all data. Errors (if any) are consistent across all rows, making them easier to spot and fix.

** Keeping Local Options Open: Ollama for Private Models
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:ollama
:END:
#+Latex:\index{Ollama}
\index{local LLM}
\index{privacy!local models}

Ollama is a tool for running open-source language models (LLaMA, Mistral, others) locally on your machine. For sensitive manuscripts (before publication, under embargo, proprietary), consider local models.

*** Advantages of Local Models
\index{AI agents!local models!advantages}

1. Privacy: Your text never leaves your machine.
2. Control: You choose the model and can update it.
3. Availability: Works offline.
4. Cost: No API fees.

*** Disadvantages
\index{AI agents!local models!disadvantages}

1. Quality: Open-source models are generally less capable than Claude or GPT-4.
2. Speed: Runs on your hardware; slower than cloud models.
3. Setup: Requires installation and configuration.

*** When to Use Local Models
\index{AI agents!local models!when to use}

Use local models when:
- Your manuscript contains sensitive information (unpublished data, identifiable human subjects)
- You are under embargo and cannot use cloud APIs
- You have weak internet connectivity
- You want to minimize API costs

*** Trade-offs
\index{AI agents!local models!tradeoffs}

Local models are a reasonable fallback but are not ready to replace Claude or GPT-4 for high-stakes writing. Accept reduced quality in exchange for privacy.

** AI Agent Dos for Foundations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:foundation-dos
:END:
\index{AI agents!foundations!dos}

1. Ask the AI to explain the Model Context Protocol in plain language so you understand what external databases are available.
2. Have the AI help you set up MCP servers for your field (PubMed for biomedical work, ArXiv for physics/CS).
3. Use the AI to write a Python script that tests your MCP connection by performing a sample search.
4. Ask the AI to explain context window constraints and how to estimate token usage for your project.
5. Have the AI create a "story so far" template customized to your project structure.
6. Ask the AI to recommend a folder structure for your manuscript project (chapters, figures, tables, code, grounding).
7. Have the AI help you draft a workflow document describing your Cowork vs. Code decision for each task type.
8. Ask the AI to explain the permission model and why it matters for safety.

** AI Agent Do-Nots for Foundations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:foundation-do-nots
:END:
\index{AI agents!foundations!do-nots}

1. Do not assume the AI understands your institution's security policies. Verify that cloud APIs are permitted.
2. Do not use cloud APIs for embargoed or classified manuscripts without explicit approval from your institution.
3. Do not trust the AI to set up MCP servers correctly without testing a sample query.
4. Do not assume local models are private. Some may phone home for updates; verify documentation.
5. Do not skip the permission gate. Always review and approve AI-proposed file operations.

** AI Verification Chores for Foundations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:foundation-verification
:END:
\index{AI agents!foundations!verification}

1. Test your MCP connection by searching for a well-known recent paper (within the last 6 months). Confirm the AI retrieves it.
2. Run a sample query on your local model (if using Ollama) and verify the output quality is acceptable.
3. Review your institution's cloud API usage policy to confirm Claude Code use is permitted.
4. Create a test "story so far" document and verify the AI can read and understand it.

* Phase 1: Project Initiation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: phase1-initiation
:END:
#+Latex:\index{project initiation}
\index{AI agents!project initiation}
\index{manuscript assembly!project initiation}

Project initiation is the foundation of the entire manuscript.
In a single extended session (typically three to four hours), you articulate the central question, identify the audience, survey target journals, draft preliminary introductory paragraphs, list potential results and discussion points, brainstorm titles and keywords, draft an abstract, and begin the cover letter.
The quality of this phase determines how clearly you can maintain momentum throughout the project.


** Central Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:central
:END:
#+Latex:\index{central question}
\index{AI agents!central question}

The central question or hypothesis is the nucleus of the paper.
Every section of the manuscript should orbit around it.
Getting the wording right usually requires testing six or more trial sentences.

*** AI Agent Dos for Central Question
\index{AI agents!central question!dos}

1. Generate ten or more alternative phrasings of your draft central question to expose hidden assumptions and sharpen the scope.
2. Ask the AI to identify the independent and dependent variables implied by each candidate phrasing.
3. Have the AI map each candidate question to the experimental or computational approaches that would be needed to answer it.
4. Request a brief literature scan to determine whether the question has already been answered (and by whom).
5. Ask the AI to rewrite the question at three levels of specificity (broad, medium, narrow) so you can choose the appropriate scope.
6. Use the AI to generate a one-paragraph "elevator pitch" for each candidate question, which can reveal which version is most compelling.
7. Ask the AI to identify the null hypothesis and the alternative hypothesis for each candidate.
8. Have the AI flag any candidate questions that are unfalsifiable or too vague to test.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Central Question
\index{AI agents!central question!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI choose your central question for you. The question must originate from your expertise, your data, and your intellectual curiosity.
2. Do not trust the AI's claim that a question is "novel." The AI cannot reliably assess the full state of the literature because it lacks access to paywalled content, preprints posted after its training cutoff, and unpublished work.
3. Do not accept AI-generated phrasings that sound impressive but obscure the testable claim. Clarity beats elegance.
4. Do not use the AI to reverse-engineer a question from a desired conclusion. That is confirmation bias automated.
5. Do not rely on the AI's assessment of feasibility. Only you know your lab's capabilities, resources, and timeline.
6. Do not let AI-suggested jargon creep into the question. If you would not use a term in conversation with a colleague, remove it.

*** AI Verification Chores for Central Question
\index{AI agents!central question!verification}

1. Read every AI-suggested phrasing aloud to a colleague or to yourself. If it does not make immediate sense, discard it.
2. Cross-check any AI claim about literature novelty with a direct PubMed or Google Scholar search using the key terms from the question.
3. Confirm that the AI-suggested variables are the ones you actually measure or manipulate in your study.
4. Verify that the question is falsifiable by asking yourself: "What result would disprove this?"


** Rationale
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:why
:END:
#+Latex:\index{rationale}
\index{AI agents!rationale}

*** AI Agent Dos for Rationale
\index{AI agents!rationale!dos}

1. Ask the AI to summarize the current state of the subfield in two to three paragraphs, highlighting the gap your work fills.
2. Have the AI extract "future directions" paragraphs from five to ten recent papers in your area to see what the community considers unresolved.
3. Use the AI to draft a funding-agency-friendly rationale paragraph (e.g., for NIH significance criteria) so you can repurpose it later.
4. Ask the AI to list three to five stakeholders (clinicians, engineers, policymakers) who would benefit from the answer to your question.
5. Have the AI compare your rationale to those in recently published papers in your target journals to calibrate tone and depth.
6. Ask the AI to identify any logical gaps or unstated assumptions in your draft rationale.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Rationale
\index{AI agents!rationale!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI fabricate a rationale that overstates the clinical or societal impact. Reviewers and editors detect hype.
2. Do not accept citations the AI provides without verifying that the cited papers exist, say what the AI claims they say, and have not been retracted.
3. Do not let the AI conflate your rationale with a generic "this topic is important" statement. The rationale must be specific to your study.
4. Do not use AI to generate a rationale for a project you cannot articulate yourself. If you cannot explain why the project matters, it may not be ready.

*** AI Verification Chores for Rationale
\index{AI agents!rationale!verification}

1. Verify every citation the AI includes by checking the DOI or PubMed ID.
2. Read the abstract of every cited paper to confirm it supports the claim made in the rationale.
3. Check for retracted papers using Retraction Watch or PubMed's retraction filter.
4. Ask a colleague unfamiliar with the project to read the rationale and summarize what they think the project is about. If their summary diverges, rewrite.


** Audience
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:audience
:END:
#+Latex:\index{audience for the paper}
\index{AI agents!audience identification}

*** AI Agent Dos for Audience
\index{AI agents!audience!dos}

1. Ask the AI to characterize the readership of your top three target journals based on recent publication topics.
2. Have the AI identify interdisciplinary audiences who might find your work relevant and suggest how to frame the paper to reach them.
3. Use the AI to generate a "jargon budget"---a list of technical terms you can use without definition versus those that need explanation for your audience.
4. Ask the AI to compare the reading level (Flesch-Kincaid) of your draft prose against recent articles in the target journal.
5. Have the AI draft one paragraph of the introduction at two different levels of assumed background knowledge so you can choose the right pitch.
6. Ask the AI to identify professional societies or conferences whose attendees overlap with your target audience.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Audience
\index{AI agents!audience!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI default to "scientists in general" as your audience. Specificity matters.
2. Do not accept the AI's assumptions about what your audience knows. You know your community better than the AI does.
3. Do not use AI readability scores as the sole arbiter of appropriate complexity. Some fields have higher baseline complexity.

*** AI Verification Chores for Audience
\index{AI agents!audience!verification}

1. Scan the author affiliations and keywords of the last twenty papers in your target journal to confirm your audience characterization.
2. Check that the jargon budget aligns with the terminology used in recent articles in the target journal.


** Target Journals
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:target-journals
:END:
#+Latex:\index{target journals}
\index{AI agents!target journals}

*** AI Agent Dos for Target Journals
\index{AI agents!target journals!dos}

1. Ask the AI to compare your manuscript's scope, anticipated length, and reference count against recent publications in each candidate journal.
2. Have the AI retrieve each journal's impact factor, acceptance rate, and typical time to first decision (where publicly available).
3. Use the AI to extract submission guidelines (word limits, figure caps, required sections, reference style) for each candidate journal.
4. Ask the AI to identify journals that have published similar studies in the past two years by searching for your keywords.
5. Have the AI check whether each candidate journal charges open-access fees and whether your funder's mandate is compatible.
6. Ask the AI to draft a brief comparison table of your top four journals with columns for scope match, impact factor, open access policy, and turnaround time.
7. Have the AI check whether your intended reference style (BibTeX vs. BibLaTeX, numbered vs. author-year) matches each journal.
8. Ask the AI to identify any special issues or themed collections at candidate journals that align with your topic.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Target Journals
\index{AI agents!target journals!do-nots}

1. Do not trust the AI's impact factor or acceptance rate numbers without verifying them against the journal's website or Journal Citation Reports. These numbers change annually.
2. Do not let the AI recommend predatory journals. Cross-check any unfamiliar journal against Beall's list or the Directory of Open Access Journals (DOAJ).
3. Do not rely on the AI's judgment about "scope match." Only you and the journal editors know whether the work fits.
4. Do not assume the AI has current information about journal policies; guidelines change frequently.

*** AI Verification Chores for Target Journals
\index{AI agents!target journals!verification}

1. Visit each candidate journal's website and confirm the submission guidelines the AI retrieved.
2. Verify impact factors via Journal Citation Reports or Scopus.
3. Check DOAJ for legitimate open-access journals.
4. Confirm open-access fees and funder compatibility on the journal's author information page.


** Related Projects
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:related-projects
:END:
#+Latex:\index{related projects}
\index{AI agents!related projects}

*** AI Agent Dos for Related Projects
\index{AI agents!related projects!dos}

1. Ask the AI to identify synergies between your current project and your other active manuscripts by summarizing shared datasets, methods, or themes.
2. Have the AI suggest how reading done for this project could be leveraged for a review article or book chapter.
3. Use the AI to generate a dependency graph showing which of your projects share data, reagents, or computational tools.
4. Ask the AI to flag potential self-plagiarism risks if you are working on closely related manuscripts.
5. Have the AI draft a paragraph describing how this project extends or complements a prior publication from your group.
6. Ask the AI to identify potential co-submission opportunities (e.g., a methods paper and an applications paper).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Related Projects
\index{AI agents!related projects!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI conflate distinct projects. Each manuscript must have its own central question and contribution.
2. Do not use AI to auto-generate text shared across manuscripts. Even paraphrased overlap can trigger plagiarism detectors.
3. Do not trust the AI to know which of your projects are active, funded, or prioritized. This is your domain knowledge.

*** AI Verification Chores for Related Projects
\index{AI agents!related projects!verification}

1. Run your draft text through a plagiarism detector (e.g., iThenticate) to catch unintentional overlap with your own prior work.
2. Confirm that the dependency graph matches your actual data and tool sharing by consulting your lab records.


** Draft Introduction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:Introduction
:END:
#+Latex:\index{draft!introduction}
\index{AI agents!draft introduction}

*** AI Agent Dos for Draft Introduction
\index{AI agents!draft introduction!dos}

1. Ask the AI to synthesize a two-paragraph funnel-shaped introduction from your literature notes, starting broad and narrowing to your specific aims.
2. Have the AI identify the three to five most-cited foundational references for your subfield and draft sentences situating your work relative to them.
3. Use the AI to generate transition sentences between the "state of the field" paragraph and the "gap in knowledge" paragraph.
4. Ask the AI to check whether your introduction implicitly promises results that you do not deliver in the manuscript.
5. Have the AI flag any claims in the introduction that lack a supporting citation.
6. Ask the AI to rewrite your introduction at 80% of its current length to tighten the prose.
7. Have the AI generate three alternative opening sentences so you can choose the most engaging hook.
8. Ask the AI to verify that every abbreviation introduced in the introduction is defined at first use.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Draft Introduction
\index{AI agents!draft introduction!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI write the entire introduction from scratch without your outline. The narrative arc must reflect your understanding of the field.
2. Do not accept AI-generated citations without checking them. AI frequently hallucinates references---inventing plausible-sounding but nonexistent papers.
3. Do not let the AI overstate the novelty of your work. Reviewers who work in your field will know better.
4. Do not let the AI insert hedging language ("it is widely believed that...") where a direct statement with a citation would be stronger.
5. Do not use the AI to pad the introduction with tangential background. Every sentence must serve the funnel toward your central question.

*** AI Verification Chores for Draft Introduction
\index{AI agents!draft introduction!verification}

1. Verify every citation by looking up the DOI. If a citation does not resolve, the AI invented it.
2. Read the abstract of every cited paper to confirm it says what the introduction claims.
3. Check for retracted papers.
4. Ask a colleague to read the introduction and identify the central question based solely on what they read. If they cannot, the funnel is broken.

6. Ask the AI to check for weasel words, wordy phrases, and cliches in the text you wrote during the session.
7. Have the AI generate the Hemingway bridge for the end of the session: summarize current state, list next actions, and record fleeting details.
8. Use the AI to update the word count log and generate the cumulative word count plot data.
9. Ask the AI to extract TODO items from your session notes and format them as org-mode TODO entries.
10. Have the AI spell-check and grammar-check the text you wrote during the session.
11. Ask the AI to update your abbreviation list if new terms were introduced during the session.
12. Have the AI scan for any new references you cited and verify their BibTeX entries exist in your bibliography file.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Daily Writing Sessions
\index{AI agents!daily writing!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI do your generative writing. Generative writing is the most valuable activity, and it must come from your brain. Use AI for polishing, not originating.
2. Do not let the AI make decisions about what to write next. You set the priorities; the AI helps execute.
3. Do not accept AI rewrites without reading them carefully. The AI sometimes changes scientific meaning while "improving" style.
4. Do not let the AI remove hedging language where uncertainty is scientifically appropriate. "May" and "suggests" are sometimes the honest words.
5. Do not use the AI as a procrastination tool. If you spend the session prompting the AI instead of writing, the AI is hurting your productivity.
6. Do not let the AI introduce English contractions. Contractions are inappropriate in scientific prose.

*** AI Verification Chores for Daily Writing Sessions
\index{AI agents!daily writing!verification}

1. Read every AI-rewritten sentence aloud. If the meaning has shifted, revert to the original and rewrite manually.
2. Check that the AI did not introduce any new citations. If it did, verify them.
3. Compile the document after each session to catch any syntax errors introduced by AI edits.
4. Review the Hemingway bridge to ensure it accurately captures your mental state and plans.

The /rewritechapter skill provides a structured, human-in-the-loop workflow for drafting book chapters. It decomposes the drafting process into six distinct phases, each with a human verification gate.

*** Phase 1 (Setup)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!setup}

Initialize the project and assemble grounding documents. Inputs:
- Chapter brief (scope, outline, key sections)
- Literature summaries or grounding documents
- Data descriptions or prior results
- Analysis plan (if applicable)

Output: Project context document summarizing what will be drafted.

*** Phase 2 (Outline)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!outline}

Create detailed chapter structure and section headings. The AI generates a hierarchical outline (typically five to eight main sections, each with two to four subsections) based on your chapter brief. You review and revise the outline before proceeding.

*** Phase 3 (Section-by-Section)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!section-by-section}

Draft content for each section sequentially. The AI produces approximately 500 to 800 words per section, incorporating citations and grounded in your provided materials. You review and approve each section before the AI proceeds to the next section. This phase takes the most time but ensures quality at each step.

*** Phase 4 (Equation Verification)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!equation verification}

Check all mathematical expressions, variable definitions, and LaTeX syntax. The AI extracts all equations, verifies that notation is consistent, and checks that each equation compiles correctly in LaTeX. You confirm that equations match your intent.

*** Phase 5 (Chapter Review)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!chapter review}

Full chapter review before finalization. The AI generates a summary of the chapter's arguments and a checklist of structural elements. You perform final edits.

*** Phase 6 (Citation Finalization)
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!citation finalization}

Validate all citations. The AI extracts every citation key, verifies that the BibTeX entry exists, and generates a brief report of any missing entries. You resolve any citation gaps.

*** Advantages of the /rewritechapter Workflow
\index{AI agents!rewritechapter!advantages}

1. Structured progression prevents skipping critical steps.
2. Human gates at each phase catch errors early.
3. Section-by-section review ensures grounding in your materials.
4. Built-in citation validation prevents hallucinated references.
5. Suitable for chapters where you must maintain authorial voice.

** Grounding Documents
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:grounding-docs
:END:
#+Latex:\index{grounding documents}
\index{AI agents!grounding documents}

Grounding documents are materials you provide to the AI to anchor its responses in facts rather than patterns from training data. They function as a safeguard against hallucination.

*** Types of Grounding Documents
\index{AI agents!grounding documents!types}

1. *Literature summaries*: Bullet-pointed extracts from key papers, including author, year, and main finding.
2. *Data descriptions*: Summaries of datasets you analyzed, including sample size, variables, and source.
3. *Prior results*: Findings from your own prior work that contextualize the current project.
4. *Analysis plans*: Descriptions of statistical methods you will use and why.
5. *Protocol exports*: Electronic lab notebook entries converted to structured text.
6. *Comparison tables*: Side-by-side tables comparing your findings to those of others.

*** Three-Question Decision Tree
\index{AI agents!grounding documents!decision tree}

When deciding whether to provide a grounding document:

1. *Is this within the AI's training data scope?* (Likely hallucination risk if yes—consider grounding)
2. *Does this require exact facts or citations?* (Definitely needs grounding)
3. *Is there uncertainty about correctness?* (Use grounding)

If all three are "yes," provide a grounding document. If only one is "yes," grounding is optional but recommended.

*** Purpose and Effective Use
\index{AI agents!grounding documents!effective use}

The purpose of grounding is to *bind the AI's output to verifiable facts*. Effective grounding:
- Is explicitly referenced in your prompt ("According to the grounding document you provided...")
- Includes specific passages the AI should cite
- Identifies the source and year of each fact
- Contains no fabricated information (verify it yourself first)

*** Limitations
\index{AI agents!grounding documents!limitations}

Grounding does not guarantee accuracy. The AI can still misrepresent grounded material. Verification remains mandatory.

** Context Management Techniques
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:context-management
:END:
#+Latex:\index{context management}
\index{AI agents!context management}

As manuscripts grow, the AI's context window (the amount of text the AI can attend to in a single conversation) becomes constrained. Strategic context management extends your effective working session length.

*** Context Compression
\index{AI agents!context management!compression}

When approaching the token limit, compress prior conversation into a summary. Instead of including the full prior messages, provide:
- A one-paragraph summary of the chapter's scope and argument
- A list of decisions made so far (e.g., "We decided to emphasize X over Y")
- A list of outstanding issues or unknowns

*** Progressive Disclosure
\index{AI agents!context management!progressive disclosure}

Introduce information in phases rather than all at once. For example, when drafting a multi-section chapter:
- Phase 1: Provide introduction materials and draft the introduction
- Phase 2: Provide methods materials and draft the methods
- Phase 3: Provide results and draft the results
- Phase 4: Provide literature for discussion and draft discussion

This keeps the context window from becoming cluttered with information not yet needed.

*** "Story So Far" Document
\index{AI agents!context management!story so far}

When you must reset the context window (after drafting a chapter), create a "Story So Far" summary:

#+BEGIN_VERSE
CHAPTER TITLE: [Your Chapter Title]
CENTRAL ARGUMENT: [One sentence summary of the chapter's main claim]
SECTIONS COMPLETED:
1. Introduction - status: [COMPLETE/IN PROGRESS/PENDING]
2. Methods - status: [COMPLETE/IN PROGRESS/PENDING]
   [Continue for all sections]

KEY DECISIONS:
- [Decision 1]: [Rationale]
- [Decision 2]: [Rationale]

OUTSTANDING ISSUES:
- [Issue 1]: [Status]
- [Issue 2]: [Status]

NEXT ACTION: [Specific task to resume with]
#+END_VERSE

Pass this document to the AI at the start of the next session. It provides full context without consuming excessive tokens.

*** When to Reset
\index{AI agents!context management!when to reset}

Reset the context window when:
- You have completed a major section or chapter
- The conversation thread has grown very long (approaching 50,000 tokens)
- You are moving to a different type of task (e.g., from drafting to verification)
- Multiple days have passed and you want to ensure continuity

*** Continuity Across Resets
\index{AI agents!context management!continuity}

After a context reset:
1. Provide the "Story So Far" document at the very start
2. Reiterate your current goal (e.g., "Continue drafting the Discussion section")
3. Ask the AI to confirm it understands the prior context before proceeding
4. Provide any new materials needed for the current task

** Human-in-the-Loop Gates

#+Latex:\index{human-in-the-loop gates}
\index{AI agents!human-in-the-loop gates}

Human review of AI output is not optional—it is the core of responsible AI-assisted writing. Implement three strategies for human verification.

*** Inline Review
\index{AI agents!human-in-the-loop gates!inline}

Review each section immediately after the AI drafts it, before proceeding to the next section. Advantages:
- Errors caught early, preventing cascading problems
- Context is fresh (you remember the source materials)
- Course corrections can be applied immediately

Disadvantages:
- Time-consuming (extends the drafting process)
- Requires continuous attention and decision-making

*** Batch Review
\index{AI agents!human-in-the-loop gates!batch}

Draft multiple sections, then review all at once. Advantages:
- Faster initial drafting (less switching between drafting and reviewing)
- Consistent revisions across sections
- Efficiency gains from batch processing

Disadvantages:
- Errors propagate across multiple sections before being caught
- Context fades between drafting and review

*** Hybrid Strategy
\index{AI agents!human-in-the-loop gates!hybrid}

Perform section-level logic and accuracy checks during drafting (inline), but defer polish and style editing for a final batch review. This balances speed with error catching.

** Authorial Voice Preservation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:authorial-voice
:END:
#+Latex:\index{authorial voice}
\index{AI agents!authorial voice}

The ICMJE authorship standard holds that an author must contribute to the conception or design of the work or to the interpretation of results. If the AI writes the entire paper, who is the author? The answer is: the human is the author if they made substantive decisions about what to say and how to say it.

*** ICMJE Standard
\index{AI agents!authorial voice!ICMJE}

The International Committee of Medical Journal Editors defines authorship as requiring:
1. Substantial contributions to conception or design of work, or acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data
2. Drafting the work or critical revision for important intellectual content
3. Final approval of the version to be published
4. Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work

Drafting with AI assistance does not violate these criteria if the human is responsible for items 1, 2, 3, and 4.

*** What the Author Contributes
\index{AI agents!authorial voice!human contribution}

The human author must:
- Select which evidence to present and in what order
- Choose which prior work to emphasize and which to minimize
- Approve the overall argument and narrative structure
- Revise for accuracy, clarity, and consistency with their interpretive perspective
- Decide on the tone and emphasis (e.g., cautious vs. confident, broad vs. specialized)

The AI can:
- Generate candidate paragraphs or sections
- Draft figures and tables from data
- Suggest alternative phrasings
- Verify formatting and citations

*** Maintaining Distinctive Voice
\index{AI agents!authorial voice!maintaining voice}

Risk: Generic academic prose when all sections are drafted by the same AI model with identical prompts. Countermeasures:

1. *Chapter brief*: Include a section on "voice and tone" describing how your writing differs from your field's typical style. Ask the AI to match this voice.
2. *Editorial revision*: After drafting, read every sentence and ask, "Does this sound like me?" Revise sentences that feel generic.
3. *Purposeful revision*: Do not accept AI drafts as-is. Revise sections to infuse your own interpretive perspective, distinctive phrasing, and unique insights.
4. *Authorial framing*: Write the introduction and discussion yourself, letting AI handle methods and results. These sections carry more voice.

* Phase 7: Systematic Verification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: phase7-verification
:END:
#+Latex:\index{systematic verification}
\index{AI agents!systematic verification}
\index{manuscript assembly!verification}

Verification is the structural defense against hallucination, misattribution, and logical error in AI-assisted writing.
Rather than a single final pass, verification operates on three layers: citation integrity, claim accuracy, and logical soundness.
This phase covers the mechanics of detecting problems at each layer and the remediation workflow.

** Citation Layer: Extraction and Audit
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:citation-layer
:END:
#+Latex:\index{citation layer!audit}
\index{AI agents!citation extraction}

The citation layer ensures that every bibliographic key in the manuscript resolves to a valid entry and that the entry is complete and correctly formatted.

*** AI Agent Dos for Citation Layer
\index{AI agents!citation layer!dos}

1. Ask the AI to extract all citation keys from the manuscript using grep or regex, reporting file names and line numbers.
2. Have the AI cross-check extracted keys against the bibliography file, flagging missing, orphaned, or duplicated entries.
3. Use the AI to categorize audit failures: missing fields, format errors, invalid characters, duplicate keys, inconsistent author names.
4. Ask the AI to validate each BibTeX entry structure (opening brace, closing brace, comma separators, field names).
5. Have the AI scan for common formatting problems: abbreviations without proper escaping, accented characters not in LaTeX format, missing DOI fields.
6. Ask the AI to identify cross-file consistency issues if the manuscript spans multiple chapter files.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Citation Layer
\index{AI agents!citation layer!do-nots}

1. Do not assume AI extraction is exhaustive. The citation key format may vary (\cite{}, \citep{}, \citet{}, custom commands).
2. Do not trust the AI to recognize valid but obscure BibTeX field names without checking against the BibTeX standard.
3. Do not let the AI auto-fix BibTeX entries without your review. Some "fixes" introduce new errors.

*** AI Verification Chores for Citation Layer
\index{AI agents!citation layer!verification}

1. Run the manuscript through LaTeX and capture the compilation log, searching for "undefined citation" warnings.
2. Use bibtool (or equivalent) to validate the .bib file: =bibtool --check manuscript.bib=.
3. Manually spot-check 20 entries from the bibliography for format consistency.
4. Verify that the number of unique citation keys matches the number reported by the AI extraction.

** DOI Resolution and Validation Pipeline
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:doi-resolution
:END:
#+Latex:\index{DOI resolution}
\index{AI agents!DOI validation}

DOI resolution bridges the bibliography entry to the actual published paper, enabling verification that the cited paper is the one you intended to cite.

*** AI Agent Dos for DOI Resolution
\index{AI agents!DOI resolution!dos}

1. Ask the AI to extract all DOI fields from the bibliography and test each one via the CrossRef MCP server (if available) or via https://doi.org/ lookup.
2. Have the AI match the returned metadata (authors, title, year, journal) against the bibliography entry, flagging discrepancies.
3. Use the AI to identify bibliography entries missing DOI fields and attempt to retrieve DOIs from CrossRef or Semantic Scholar using author and title.
4. Ask the AI to flag entries where the DOI resolves to a different paper than expected (year mismatch, title mismatch).
5. Have the AI generate a validation report with columns for key, DOI, resolution status (found/not-found/error), and any discrepancies.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for DOI Resolution
\index{AI agents!DOI resolution!do-nots}

1. Do not assume all papers have DOIs. Older papers and non-journal publications may lack them.
2. Do not let the AI modify DOI fields without your approval. Some entries intentionally lack DOIs for historical or open-access reasons.
3. Do not trust a "not found" response as definitive. The DOI may be correct but the lookup service temporarily unavailable.

*** AI Verification Chores for DOI Resolution
\index{AI agents!DOI resolution!verification}

1. Manually test 10 random DOIs by clicking them in a browser.
2. For papers flagged as "discrepancies," read the abstract to confirm what the paper actually says.
3. Check retraction databases (Retraction Watch, PubMed retraction filter) for flagged papers.

** Claim-Source Matching and the Misattribution Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:claim-source-matching
:END:
#+Latex:\index{claim-source matching}
\index{misattribution problem}
\index{AI agents!claim verification}

The misattribution problem occurs when a claim is partially supported by a cited source, fully supported by a different source, or supported by none.
Claim-source matching requires extracting each substantive claim from the manuscript, locating the cited source, and comparing.

*** AI Agent Dos for Claim-Source Matching
\index{AI agents!claim verification!dos}

1. Ask the AI to extract every factual claim from a single section and list it with the corresponding citation key.
2. Have the AI retrieve the abstract and/or full text of each cited paper (via PubMed, CrossRef, or Semantic Scholar MCP).
3. Use the AI to perform a side-by-side comparison: claim on the left, source text on the right, with a judgment of match quality (full/partial/none).
4. Ask the AI to categorize partial matches: the source supports the general claim but not the specific numbers, the source is closely related but not identical, the source contradicts the claim.
5. Have the AI prioritize claims for manual verification: critical findings first, supporting claims later.
6. Ask the AI to generate a prioritized list of problematic citations for you to review and revise.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Claim-Source Matching
\index{AI agents!claim verification!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI declare a match "good enough" when it is actually partial. Partial matches must be reworked as claims.
2. Do not skip verification of claims that seem obviously true. Well-known claims are often misattributed.
3. Do not let the AI proceed without retrieving the actual source text. Abstracts are insufficient for verification.

*** AI Verification Chores for Claim-Source Matching
\index{AI agents!claim verification!verification}

1. For each flagged claim, read the cited source in full (not just abstract).
2. If a partial match, rewrite the claim to accurately represent what the source says or add a different citation.
3. For each critical claim, consult at least two independent sources.

** Hallucinated Reference Detection: The Near-Miss Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:hallucinated-refs
:END:
#+Latex:\index{hallucinated references}
\index{near-miss problem}
\index{AI agents!hallucination detection}

Hallucinated references are invented papers or papers with fabricated details (wrong author, wrong year, wrong title).
The near-miss problem is especially insidious: a real paper with one or two details wrong.

*** AI Agent Dos for Hallucinated Reference Detection
\index{AI agents!hallucination detection!dos}

1. Ask the AI to take every citation from the bibliography and search PubMed, Google Scholar, and CrossRef using the exact title.
2. Have the AI report whether each search returns a result, and if so, whether the returned paper matches the bibliography entry (author, year, journal).
3. Use the AI to flag entries where the search returns nothing. These are candidates for hallucinations.
4. Ask the AI to perform a secondary search using author name + year for flagged entries to catch near-miss cases (same paper, wrong author or year).
5. Have the AI generate a report of suspicious entries requiring manual investigation.
6. Ask the AI to apply the practical heuristic: search for title + first author surname. This combination is nearly unique for each paper.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Hallucinated Reference Detection
\index{AI agents!hallucination detection!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that a search returning no results means the paper is hallucinated. Some legitimate papers are difficult to find.
2. Do not let the AI remove bibliography entries based solely on search failures. You must manually verify before deletion.
3. Do not trust author name variants as a reason to dismiss a "near miss." Authors legitimately use variant forms of their names.

*** AI Verification Chores for Hallucinated Reference Detection
\index{AI agents!hallucination detection!verification}

1. For every entry flagged as suspicious, attempt a direct search in PubMed/Google Scholar yourself using the title.
2. If a paper cannot be found after thorough searching, ask a colleague whether they know of it.
3. If still not found, either remove the citation or revise it to an actual paper that makes the same point.
4. Maintain a log of "suspected hallucinations found and corrected" to learn how many AI-generated citations are unreliable.

** Logical Analysis Using Claude
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:logical-analysis
:END:
#+Latex:\index{logical analysis}
\index{AI agents!logical analysis}

Logical analysis searches for structural defects in reasoning: circular reasoning, unsupported leaps, unwarranted contradictions, and false dichotomies.

*** AI Agent Dos for Logical Analysis
\index{AI agents!logical analysis!dos}

1. Ask the AI to review a section and identify the main claims, the evidence supporting each, and the logical transitions between them.
2. Have the AI flag any claims that lack supporting evidence or citations.
3. Use the AI to identify logical fallacies: circular reasoning, begging the question, false dichotomy, post-hoc reasoning, slippery slope.
4. Ask the AI to check for contradictions between statements within the section.
5. Have the AI verify that conditional statements ("if X, then Y") are justified by the preceding text.
6. Ask the AI to evaluate whether the conclusion follows from the evidence presented.
7. Have the AI check for unsupported leaps: when multiple steps of reasoning are compressed into one sentence.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Logical Analysis
\index{AI agents!logical analysis!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that every "logical issue" the AI identifies is actually a problem. Some apparent contradictions are nuanced discussions.
2. Do not let the AI require absolute proof for every claim. Scientific writing tolerates well-reasoned uncertainty.
3. Do not use logical analysis to flatten legitimate debate. Some disagreement in the literature is genuine, not erroneous.

*** AI Verification Chores for Logical Analysis
\index{AI agents!logical analysis!verification}

1. For each flagged logical issue, read the original section and judge whether the AI's concern is valid.
2. If the AI identifies a contradiction, read both statements in full context before deciding whether they actually contradict.
3. For complex arguments, ask a subject-matter expert colleague whether the logic holds.

** Sub-Agent Verification: Dual-Review Pattern
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:sub-agent-verification
:END:
#+Latex:\index{sub-agent verification}
\index{dual-review pattern}
\index{AI agents!peer review simulation}

Sub-agent verification uses independent Claude instances (or different prompt configurations) to review the same content, identifying issues that a single review might miss.

*** AI Agent Dos for Sub-Agent Verification
\index{AI agents!sub-agent verification!dos}

1. Ask Claude instance A to review Section X for logical soundness, using a structured prompt asking specifically about argument structure.
2. Ask Claude instance B (in a separate session) to review the same section using a different prompt asking about claim-source alignment.
3. Have a third instance (C) review both feedback sets and synthesize findings.
4. Ask the AI to highlight issues identified by multiple instances (higher confidence) and issues found by only one (lower confidence).
5. Use the multi-instance approach especially for critical sections (abstract, main findings, conclusions).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Sub-Agent Verification
\index{AI agents!sub-agent verification!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that consensus among multiple instances means correctness. All instances are based on the same training data.
2. Do not apply sub-agent verification to routine content. The cost (multiple session tokens) exceeds the benefit.
3. Do not let sub-agent disagreement paralyze you. Use it as a signal to read the section yourself.

*** AI Verification Chores for Sub-Agent Verification
\index{AI agents!sub-agent verification!verification}

1. For any issue identified by multiple instances, make a note and decide whether to revise.
2. For issues identified by only one instance, use your own judgment about whether to act on them.

** Domain-Specific Verification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:domain-specific-verification
:END:
#+Latex:\index{domain-specific verification}
\index{AI agents!domain-specific claims}

Certain types of claims require domain-specific expertise and verification methods beyond general logical analysis.

*** AI Agent Dos for Domain-Specific Verification
\index{AI agents!domain-specific verification!dos}

1. For statistical values: ask the AI to extract all p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes, and sample sizes; verify against the results section and original analysis.
2. For chemical/biological identifiers: ask the AI to validate chemical formulas (e.g., match to PubChem), gene names (against NCBI Gene database), species names (against taxonomic databases).
3. For dates/names/historical claims: ask the AI to cross-check against archival sources or historical databases.
4. For molecular structures: ask the AI to verify SMILES strings or structure representations against chemical databases.
5. Have the AI identify statements that require domain expertise for verification and flag them for human review.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Domain-Specific Verification
\index{AI agents!domain-specific verification!do-nots}

1. Do not rely on the AI alone for verification of specialized technical content. Consult domain experts.
2. Do not assume that the AI knows the correct format for chemical formulas, gene names, or other standardized identifiers.
3. Do not let the AI "auto-correct" technical identifiers without your approval.

*** AI Verification Chores for Domain-Specific Verification
\index{AI agents!domain-specific verification!verification}

1. For statistical claims, recalculate or retrieve the original source values.
2. For biological identifiers, consult the appropriate database (NCBI, EMBL, UniProt) directly.
3. For historical claims, verify via primary sources or established reference works.

** Verification Reports: Structure and Use
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:verification-reports
:END:
#+Latex:\index{verification reports}
\index{AI agents!verification reporting}

A verification report captures all findings from the three-layer verification process and prioritizes them for revision.

*** AI Agent Dos for Verification Reports
\index{AI agents!verification reports!dos}

1. Ask the AI to compile all findings from citation audit, claim-source matching, hallucination detection, and logical analysis into a single report.
2. Have the AI organize findings by section and by issue type (citation, claim, logic, domain-specific).
3. Use the AI to assign a confidence level (High = definite error, Medium = likely error, Low = possible issue) to each finding.
4. Ask the AI to prioritize findings by impact: issues affecting main conclusions first, supporting details later.
5. Have the AI generate a summary table with columns: issue type, section, description, confidence, suggested action.
6. Ask the AI to distinguish between "must fix" (accuracy errors) and "should fix" (logical improvements).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Verification Reports
\index{AI agents!verification reports!do-nots}

1. Do not treat all findings equally. Confidence levels matter; a low-confidence issue may not warrant revision.
2. Do not let the AI decide what constitutes an error. Some "issues" may reflect your intentional rhetorical choices.
3. Do not generate verification reports if you have not yet identified issues through actual verification. A report without evidence is merely speculation.

*** AI Verification Chores for Verification Reports
\index{AI agents!verification reports!verification}

1. Review the confidence assignments. Do they match your own judgment of severity?
2. For high-confidence issues, immediately revise the manuscript.
3. For medium-confidence issues, decide whether to revise based on the section's importance.
4. Archive the verification report with your manuscript for future reference and during submission (if journals request verification documentation).

* Phase 8: Comprehensive Revision
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: phase8-revision
:END:
#+Latex:\index{comprehensive revision}
\index{AI agents!manuscript revision}
\index{manuscript assembly!revision}

Revision builds on the verification phase by addressing not only errors but also structural clarity, logical strength, stylistic consistency, and readability.
The key principle is top-down: resolve large-scale issues before copy-editing details.

** Three Levels of Editing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:three-levels-editing
:END:
#+Latex:\index{developmental editing}
\index{copyediting}
\index{proofreading}
\index{AI agents!three levels of editing}

Developmental editing addresses large-scale structural and logical issues.
Copyediting focuses on clarity, concision, consistency, and style.
Proofreading catches typos, formatting errors, and final polish.
Do not proceed to the next level until the current level is complete.

*** AI Agent Dos for Three-Level Editing
\index{AI agents!three-level editing!dos}

1. At the developmental level: ask the AI to create a structural outline of the current manuscript and identify sections that are too long, too short, logically disconnected, or redundant.
2. At the copyediting level: ask the AI to scan for clarity issues, jargon requiring definition, passive voice that obscures agency, and inconsistent terminology.
3. At the proofreading level: ask the AI to identify typos, spelling errors, punctuation errors, formatting inconsistencies, and incorrect cross-references.
4. Have the AI generate a separate report for each level, to be addressed sequentially.
5. Use the AI to verify that each level of revision is complete before moving to the next.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Three-Level Editing
\index{AI agents!three-level editing!do-nots}

1. Do not skip developmental editing to focus on copyediting. Fixing typos in a poorly structured section is wasted effort.
2. Do not let the AI mix concerns across levels. Keep structural feedback separate from style feedback.
3. Do not attempt all three levels in a single pass. Multi-level revision introduces contradictions.

*** AI Verification Chores for Three-Level Editing
\index{AI agents!three-level editing!verification}

1. After developmental editing, verify structural changes improve logical flow by reading the section aloud.
2. After copyediting, scan for new inconsistencies introduced during revision.
3. After proofreading, use a second tool (e.g., LanguageTool) to cross-check.

** Structural Mapping and Word Count Analysis
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:structural-mapping
:END:
#+Latex:\index{structural mapping}
\index{word count analysis}
\index{AI agents!structural analysis}

Structural mapping reveals the current organization and identifies imbalances.

*** AI Agent Dos for Structural Mapping
\index{AI agents!structural mapping!dos}

1. Ask the AI to generate a detailed outline of the current manuscript, with heading levels and approximate word counts per section.
2. Have the AI compare the current outline against the intended outline (from the chapter brief, if one exists).
3. Use the AI to calculate the proportion of words devoted to each major section (e.g., Introduction 20%, Methods 15%, Results 40%, Discussion 25%).
4. Ask the AI to identify sections that are disproportionately long or short relative to their importance.
5. Have the AI suggest specific reorganizations or deletions to improve balance.
6. Ask the AI to flag any sections that are orphaned (disconnected from neighboring sections) or redundant.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Structural Mapping
\index{AI agents!structural mapping!do-nots}

1. Do not let structural imbalance force you to pad a weak section. If a section is short because it is weak, strengthen the content rather than lengthen it.
2. Do not assume that word count should be proportional across sections. Some sections legitimately require more detail than others.
3. Do not let the AI reorganize without your review. The current structure may reflect your intentional narrative arc.

*** AI Verification Chores for Structural Mapping
\index{AI agents!structural mapping!verification}

1. Compare the AI-generated outline against your chapter brief. Are they aligned?
2. For sections flagged as imbalanced, decide whether to rebalance or to accept the current distribution.

** Topic Gap Detection
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:topic-gaps
:END:
#+Latex:\index{topic gap detection}
\index{AI agents!gap identification}

Gap detection identifies missing content by comparing the manuscript to a template, guidelines, or related literature.

*** AI Agent Dos for Topic Gap Detection
\index{AI agents!topic gap detection!dos}

1. Ask the AI to compare your Methods section against a template of standard elements (Study Design, Participants, Procedures, Data Collection, Analysis, Sample Size Justification) and identify missing pieces.
2. Have the AI compare your Discussion section against a template (Interpretation, Comparison to Literature, Limitations, Implications, Future Directions) and flag absences.
3. Use the AI to scan similar recent papers (in your target journal) and extract topics they cover in the Discussion that your manuscript does not.
4. Ask the AI to check whether your manuscript explicitly addresses the "so what?" question: why do these findings matter?
5. Have the AI verify that every claim in the Abstract is supported by content in the manuscript body.
6. Ask the AI to identify any implicit promises in the Introduction that the Results and Discussion do not deliver.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Topic Gap Detection
\index{AI agents!topic gap detection!do-nots}

1. Do not assume every gap requires filling. Some sections intentionally omit details to stay within space limits.
2. Do not let the AI pad the Discussion with generic content just to fill gaps. Content must be specific and meaningful.
3. Do not let gap detection become a form of feature creep. Resist the urge to add everything mentioned in other papers.

*** AI Verification Chores for Topic Gap Detection
\index{AI agents!topic gap detection!verification}

1. For each identified gap, decide whether it represents missing content or intentional brevity.
2. If you choose to fill a gap, verify that the new content is specific and relevant.

** Logical Strengthening: Paragraph Anatomy and Weakness Patterns
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:logical-strengthening
:END:
#+Latex:\index{logical strengthening}
\index{paragraph anatomy}
\index{AI agents!argument strengthening}

Logical strengthening focuses on improving the coherence and persuasiveness of arguments within paragraphs and across sections.

*** AI Agent Dos for Logical Strengthening
\index{AI agents!logical strengthening!dos}

1. Ask the AI to analyze a single paragraph and identify: topic sentence, evidence, analysis, conclusion.
2. Have the AI flag paragraphs missing a clear topic sentence or conclusion.
3. Use the AI to identify logical gaps within paragraphs: where evidence does not directly support the claim, where a transition is missing between ideas.
4. Ask the AI to check for evidence-to-claim ratios: paragraphs with many citations but weak conclusions, or conclusions drawn from insufficient evidence.
5. Have the AI suggest ways to strengthen weak arguments without adding speculation.
6. Ask the AI to verify that transitions between paragraphs are explicit and logical.

*** Weakness Patterns Commonly Found
\index{weakness patterns}

- Unsupported claims: assertion without evidence or citation
- Vague references: "previous research has shown" without specifying which research
- Logical gaps: multiple steps of reasoning compressed into one sentence
- Overgeneralization: a claim made too broadly
- Hedging: "it is possible that" when the evidence is more definitive
- Orphaned citations: citation present but disconnected from the surrounding argument
- Circular reasoning: the claim restated as evidence

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Logical Strengthening
\index{AI agents!logical strengthening!do-nots}

1. Do not add evidence you have not read just because the AI suggests it might support the argument.
2. Do not transform a qualified statement ("may") into an unqualified one ("is") just because stronger language sounds more assertive.
3. Do not let the AI cut hedging language that correctly reflects the current state of evidence.

*** AI Verification Chores for Logical Strengthening
\index{AI agents!logical strengthening!verification}

1. For each strengthened argument, read the original and revised versions and verify the change is an improvement.
2. Verify every newly suggested citation by checking it actually says what the AI claims.

** When to Stop Strengthening
\index{AI agents!when to stop revision}

Manuscript revision can be indefinite. At some point, diminishing returns set in.
Ask yourself: Is the current version adequate for publication?
Does further revision materially change the message?
If not, stop.

** Style Guide Enforcement
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:style-guide
:END:
#+Latex:\index{style guide enforcement}
\index{AI agents!style consistency}

Scientific writing style varies by journal, field, and publisher.
Consistent style increases professional appearance and readability.

*** AI Agent Dos for Style Guide Enforcement
\index{AI agents!style guide enforcement!dos}

1. Ask the AI to extract the journal's or publisher's style guide rules (from instructions to authors) and create a checklist.
2. Have the AI scan the manuscript against the checklist, identifying deviations: voice (active vs. passive), number style (numerals vs. spelled-out), abbreviation rules, hyphenation, capitalization.
3. Use the AI to enforce rules specific to your field (e.g., for biomedical writing: use "do not" instead of "don't," use "because" instead of "since" for causation).
4. Ask the AI to rewrite sentences that violate style rules, showing before-and-after examples.
5. Have the AI maintain consistency in specialized terminology: is it "machine learning" or "machine-learning"? "data" (singular or plural)?

*** Before-and-After Examples
\index{style guide!examples}

- Before: "It is widely believed that data analysis is important." After: "Data analysis is critical."
- Before: "The following results were obtained." After: "We obtained the following results."
- Before: "Since RNA levels increase..." (temporal, not causal). After: "As RNA levels increase..." or "Because RNA levels increase..." (if causal).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Style Guide Enforcement
\index{AI agents!style guide enforcement!do-nots}

1. Do not let style guide compliance override scientific clarity. If a rule produces ambiguity, violate the rule.
2. Do not assume the AI knows the journal's current style rules. Always verify against the instructions to authors.
3. Do not let the AI impose a uniform voice if the manuscript deliberately shifts perspective (e.g., from author-based to reader-based prose).

*** AI Verification Chores for Style Guide Enforcement
\index{AI agents!style guide enforcement!verification}

1. Spot-check 20 random sentences for style compliance.
2. Verify that before-and-after examples actually follow the stated style rules.

** Readability Analysis
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:readability-analysis
:END:
#+Latex:\index{readability metrics}
\index{Flesch-Kincaid}
\index{Gunning Fog}
\index{AI agents!readability}

Readability metrics quantify prose clarity, though they do not capture all aspects of scientific writing quality.

*** AI Agent Dos for Readability Analysis
\index{AI agents!readability analysis!dos}

1. Ask the AI to calculate Flesch-Kincaid grade level and Gunning Fog index for the manuscript.
2. Have the AI identify sentences longer than 40 words and flag them as candidates for simplification.
3. Use the AI to scan for low-frequency words or jargon and highlight terms requiring definition.
4. Ask the AI to compare readability metrics against recent articles in the target journal.
5. Have the AI suggest specific rewrites of difficult sentences, breaking them into shorter units or removing embedded clauses.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Readability Analysis
\index{AI agents!readability analysis!do-nots}

1. Do not blindly optimize for readability metrics. Scientific writing is more complex than general writing.
2. Do not oversimplify technical language to achieve a lower grade level. Precision matters more.
3. Do not let the AI break sentences in ways that sacrifice parallelism or create ambiguity.

*** AI Verification Chores for Readability Analysis
\index{AI agents!readability analysis!verification}

1. Compare the manuscript readability against a comparable recent paper in the same journal. Is the difference material?
2. Have a colleague from the target audience read a section and assess comprehension.

** Multi-Perspective Peer Review Simulation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:multi-perspective-review
:END:
#+Latex:\index{peer review simulation}
\index{AI agents!peer review}

Simulating peer review using multiple personas exposes weaknesses from different perspectives.

*** Three Personas for Review
\index{peer review!three personas}

1. **Methods Expert**: Focuses on experimental design, statistical validity, reproducibility, and technical detail. Asks: "Could I replicate this?"
2. **Generalist Reader**: Represents someone outside the specialized subfield. Asks: "Does this make sense without jargon? Is the contribution clear?"
3. **Critical Reviewer**: Actively seeks weaknesses, alternative explanations, and overgeneralization. Asks: "What is the worst thing you could say about this work?"

*** AI Agent Dos for Multi-Perspective Review
\index{AI agents!multi-perspective review!dos}

1. Ask Claude to review the Methods section as a methods expert, using a prompt that emphasizes experimental validity and reproducibility.
2. Ask Claude to review the abstract and introduction as a generalist, using a prompt emphasizing clarity and novelty.
3. Ask Claude to review the conclusions as a critical reviewer, using a prompt emphasizing limitations and alternative interpretations.
4. Have the AI synthesize feedback from all three personas, identifying themes (issues mentioned by multiple personas are higher priority).
5. Ask the AI to prioritize feedback: critical flaws first, suggestions for improvement second.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Multi-Perspective Review
\index{AI agents!multi-perspective review!do-nots}

1. Do not treat all feedback equally. Repeated concerns are more reliable than isolated ones.
2. Do not assume that critical feedback reflects actual reviewer opinion. Simulated review is imperfect.
3. Do not respond to every piece of feedback. Some concerns may reflect limitations of AI feedback, not actual weaknesses.

*** AI Verification Chores for Multi-Perspective Review
\index{AI agents!multi-perspective review!verification}

1. For high-priority feedback items, decide whether to revise based on your own judgment.
2. Run actual peer review feedback (if available from previous submissions) against the simulated feedback. How well did simulation predict real reviewer concerns?

** Addressing Reviewer Comments
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:addressing-reviewers
:END:
#+Latex:\index{reviewer response}
\index{revision letters}
\index{AI agents!revision response}

When a manuscript is returned with reviewer comments, revision must be systematic and well-documented.

*** Three Strategies for Responses
\index{reviewer response!three strategies}

1. **Revise**: Make the change the reviewer requested. This is the safest path and required when reviewer identifies a genuine error.
2. **Defend**: Provide evidence or argument for why the current approach is correct and the reviewer's suggestion is not necessary. Use this sparingly.
3. **Clarify**: Rewrite to better explain the current approach without changing the substance. Use when the reviewer misunderstood.

*** AI Agent Dos for Addressing Reviewer Comments
\index{AI agents!revision letters!dos}

1. Ask the AI to organize all reviewer comments by topic and priority.
2. Have the AI draft a response letter template with sections for each comment, including: summary of comment, response strategy, specific revisions made.
3. Use the AI to draft revised text corresponding to each comment, showing before-and-after.
4. Ask the AI to identify comments that contradict each other (requiring careful navigation).
5. Have the AI check that every substantive comment receives a response (do not ignore comments).
6. Ask the AI to generate a summary table for reviewers showing comment, response strategy, manuscript location of revision.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Addressing Reviewer Comments
\index{AI agents!revision letters!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI write defensive responses to valid criticism. If a reviewer is right, revise.
2. Do not ignore comments. Even if you disagree, you must acknowledge them.
3. Do not let the AI make revisions that undermine your main findings just to appease reviewers.

*** AI Verification Chores for Addressing Reviewer Comments
\index{AI agents!revision letters!verification}

1. For each revision the AI suggests, verify that the change addresses the reviewer's concern.
2. Read the response letter aloud. Does it sound professional and not defensive?
3. Have a colleague review the response letter before sending.

** Journal AI Policy Landscape
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:journal-ai-policy
:END:
#+Latex:\index{journal AI policy}
\index{AI disclosure policies}
\index{AI agents!policy compliance}

Most major journals now have explicit AI use policies, but they vary significantly in scope and stringency.

*** Common Policy Elements
\index{AI policy!common elements}

- AI cannot be listed as an author
- AI use in text generation must be disclosed
- Humans remain responsible for all accuracy and ethical compliance
- Scope varies: some policies address only text, others also images and code

*** Policy Divergence Across Publishers
\index{AI policy!divergence}

- **Scope**: Nature requires disclosure in "Methods" or "Acknowledgments"; some journals require dedicated "AI disclosure" section
- **Granularity**: Some require only tool names; others require prompts, model versions, outputs reviewed
- **Image and code**: Policies differ on whether AI-generated images require disclosure, whether code generation must be disclosed

*** Major Publishers and Their Policies
\index{AI policy!major publishers}

- **Nature Portfolio**: Describe AI use in Methods or Acknowledgments; distinguish between consultation and generation
- **Science (AAAS)**: Disclose AI-generated text and images; identify specific uses
- **JAMA/AMA**: Describe tools, versions, how outputs were used and reviewed
- **PLOS**: Disclosure required in Methods; verify compliance with ICMJE guidance
- **Elsevier**: Dedicated "AI disclosure" section in supplementary materials
- **IEEE**: Recommends disclosure; specifying tools and applications strengthens credibility
- **ACM**: Requires explicit identification of AI-generated content; permits submission with proper disclosure

*** Funding Agency Policies
\index{AI policy!funding agencies}

- **NIH**: Prohibits AI-generated substantive content in specific aims and research strategy (but permits for preliminary data)
- **NSF**: Requires disclosure; intellectual content must originate with applicant (AI permitted as tool)
- **ERC/Wellcome**: Disclosure required; substance of proposal must be applicant's work

*** AI Agent Dos for Policy Compliance
\index{AI agents!policy compliance!dos}

1. Ask the AI to extract the target journal's AI policy from the instructions to authors or website.
2. Have the AI compare your manuscript's AI disclosure against the journal's requirements, identifying any gaps.
3. Use the AI to generate a draft disclosure statement specific to the journal's requirements.
4. Ask the AI to verify that the scope of disclosure matches what you actually did (do not over-disclose or under-disclose).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Policy Compliance
\index{AI agents!policy compliance!do-nots}

1. Do not assume all journals have updated AI policies. Some smaller or legacy journals may not.
2. Do not let the AI decide what qualifies as "AI use requiring disclosure." When in doubt, disclose.

*** AI Verification Chores for Policy Compliance
\index{AI agents!policy compliance!verification}

1. Verify the journal's AI policy directly on their website; do not rely on AI summaries of policy.
2. Have a colleague review your disclosure statement for completeness and accuracy.

** Format Conversion Between Journal Templates
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:format-conversion
:END:
#+Latex:\index{format conversion}
\index{template adaptation}
\index{AI agents!format conversion}

When submitting to multiple journals, or when switching target journals, format conversion transforms the manuscript from one template to another while preserving content integrity.

*** Anatomy of Format Conversion
\index{format conversion!components}

- Reference style changes (numbered vs. author-year, journal abbreviations, punctuation)
- Column structure (single vs. double column, page length)
- Figure and table placement (in-text vs. end of manuscript)
- Supplementary materials handling (inline vs. separate files)
- Author affiliation format (superscript numbers vs. footnotes)
- Section structure (some journals require different organizations)

*** Worked Example: Springer to PLOS Conversion
\index{format conversion!example}

*Citation format*: Springer uses numbered citations [1], [2]. PLOS uses author-year (Author, 2024).
*Affiliation format*: Springer uses superscript numbers on author names with footnotes. PLOS uses an "Affiliations" section.
*Figure placement*: Springer allows figures in-text. PLOS typically places figures at the end of main text.
*Supplementary materials*: Springer permits extensive supplementary files. PLOS has strict limits and requires a supplementary table of contents.

*** AI Agent Dos for Format Conversion
\index{AI agents!format conversion!dos}

1. Ask the AI to identify all format differences between the current template and the target journal template.
2. Have the AI generate an automated conversion script (or step-by-step instructions) for reference reformatting.
3. Use the AI to reformat author affiliations from one style to another.
4. Ask the AI to reorganize supplementary materials to match the target journal's requirements.
5. Have the AI update figure and table captions if the target journal has different length limits.
6. Ask the AI to verify that all cross-references remain valid after format changes.

*** Common Pitfalls in Format Conversion
\index{format conversion!pitfalls}

- Citation key mismatches: BibTeX keys referenced in text do not match entries in the .bib file after conversion
- Figure/table numbering inconsistencies: Supplementary figures numbered Fig. S1 in source but must be Fig. 1 in target journal
- Author affiliation format changes: Converting from superscript to explicit text introduces line breaks or orphaned affiliations
- Supplementary material path updates: File paths in captions must change when file locations change
- Table formatting: Some journals prohibit certain table structures; conversion may require restructuring

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Format Conversion
\index{AI agents!format conversion!do-nots}

1. Do not assume automated conversion preserves all formatting. Verify manually, especially for references.
2. Do not change scientific content during format conversion. Only change presentation.
3. Do not remove supplementary materials just because the target journal discourages them. Move them to the supplement instead.

*** AI Verification Chores for Format Conversion
\index{AI agents!format conversion!verification}

1. Compile the converted manuscript and check the LaTeX log for errors.
2. Spot-check 20 random citations to verify they converted correctly.
3. Verify that all figures, tables, and supplementary files are correctly referenced in the converted manuscript.

** Word Counting in LaTeX
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:word-counting
:END:
#+Latex:\index{word counting}
\index{detex}
\index{texcount}
\index{AI agents!word count}

Different journals count words differently. Some count every word; others exclude captions, references, or supplementary materials.

*** Methods for Word Counting
\index{word counting!methods}

**Quick method (approximate)**:
  =detex manuscript.tex | wc -w=
  Fast but counts everything, including LaTeX code fragments.

**Accurate method**:
  =texcount -inc -incbib manuscript.tex=
  Handles LaTeX macros correctly, excludes preamble, counts references.

**Definition issues**: Different journals handle figures, tables, equations, appendices inconsistently.

*** AI Agent Dos for Word Counting
\index{AI agents!word counting!dos}

1. Ask the AI to run both detex and texcount on your manuscript, reporting both counts.
2. Have the AI look up the target journal's word counting policy and verify you are within limits.
3. Use the AI to identify which sections of the manuscript are longest and suggest where cuts could be made if needed.
4. Ask the AI to count only the main text (excluding abstract, references, supplementary materials) if the journal specifies.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Word Counting
\index{AI agents!word counting!do-nots}

1. Do not trust the AI's word count without verification via independent tools.
2. Do not assume all journals count words the same way. Check the specific journal's definition.

*** AI Verification Chores for Word Counting
\index{AI agents!word counting!verification}

1. Run both detex and texcount; discrepancies indicate LaTeX-specific issues.
2. Verify the reported word count against the journal's stated limits.

*** Preprint Platforms and Their Scope
\index{preprint platforms}

\index{preprint archiving!strategy}

1. Do not submit to a preprint server without checking the target journal's policy first.

3. Do not submit a substantially different version to the journal than to the preprint. This creates confusion and potential copyright issues.

*** AI Verification Chores for Preprint Strategy

*** LaTeX Draft vs. Final Mode
\index{ifdraft!draft mode}

The =\ifdraft= mechanism allows toggling between draft and final versions:

\begin{minted}{latex}
\documentclass[draft]{article}  % or [final]

\ifdraft

\fi

*** What to Check During Proof Review
\index{proof review!checklist}

- Cross-references: All page numbers correct, all labels resolve
- Hyphenation: No awkward line breaks, no excessive hyphenation
- Headers/footers: Correct page numbers, chapter names, running headers
- Blank pages: Intentional or errors?

4. Ask the AI to generate a checklist of items to verify on each page.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Proof Review
\index{AI agents!proof review!do-nots}

1. Do not make major content changes during proof review. The proofs are meant to catch formatting errors, not conceptual problems.
2. Do not accept all suggested formatting "fixes" without understanding their impact.

\index{AI agents!proof review!verification}

1. Print the PDF or view it at 100% zoom and read through page by page.

A chapter brief is a specification document that defines scope, structure, and requirements for each chapter, serving as a contract between you and the AI.

*** Components of a Chapter Brief
\index{chapter brief!components}

1. **Title and scope**: What is this chapter about? What are its boundaries?
2. **Purpose statement**: Why does this chapter exist? What problem does it solve?
3. **Intended audience**: Who reads this chapter? What background knowledge can you assume?
4. **Learning outcomes**: After reading, what should readers understand?
5. **Outline**: Section headings and 1–2 sentence summaries of each section
6. **Estimated length**: Target word count, figure count, table count
7. **Key citations**: 5–10 essential references to cover
8. **Key figures/tables**: Data or illustrations that must appear
9. **Cross-references**: Links to other chapters or sections
10. **Style requirements**: Any chapter-specific conventions (voice, notation, terminology)
11. **Notes/caveats**: Edge cases, disputed topics, or areas requiring careful treatment

*** AI Agent Dos for Chapter Briefs
\index{AI agents!chapter briefs!dos}

1. Draft the chapter brief yourself before asking the AI to draft the chapter. The brief encodes your knowledge and intentions.
2. Ask the AI to review your draft brief and flag any ambiguities or missing information.
3. Use the AI to generate a more detailed outline from your brief, with subsections and estimated length for each.
4. Have the AI cross-check the brief against the shared resource files (acronyms, symbols, glossary) to identify terminology that needs to be used consistently.
5. Ask the AI to flag any points in the brief that might conflict with content in other chapters (if available).

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Chapter Briefs
\index{AI agents!chapter briefs!do-nots}

1. Do not let the AI write the chapter brief for you. The brief must reflect your intentions and knowledge.
2. Do not skip the chapter brief to save time. The time invested in a clear brief reduces revision time later.
3. Do not use a vague brief. "Write about statistics" is not adequate; "Introduce frequentist p-value hypothesis testing, contrast with Bayesian alternatives, explain common misinterpretations" is better.

*** AI Verification Chores for Chapter Briefs
\index{AI agents!chapter briefs!verification}

1. After drafting the brief, read it aloud to a colleague to verify clarity.
2. Cross-check the outline against recent textbooks or review articles in the same area.

** Chapter-by-Chapter Generation Workflow
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:chapter-generation-workflow
:END:
#+Latex:\index{chapter generation}
\index{one chapter per session}
\index{AI agents!chapter drafting}

Generating one chapter per AI session limits context requirements and enables clear handoff between sessions.

*** Sequencing Considerations
\index{chapter sequencing}

- Generate chapters that have no dependencies first (e.g., Introduction before Results)
- Generate foundational chapters before chapters that build on them
- Consider the reading order of the final book when sequencing generation
- Maintain a dependency graph of chapter relationships

*** One Session Per Chapter Pattern
\index{one session per chapter}

1. Read the chapter brief into the session
2. Load the shared resource files (acronyms, symbols, glossary)
3. Provide grounding documents (relevant literature summaries, data descriptions)
4. Use the /rewritechapter skill to draft the chapter
5. Review, revise, and finalize the chapter
6. Save the chapter and context summary for the next session
7. Begin next session with a "Story So Far" document summarizing prior work

*** Resuming Interrupted Work
\index{interrupted work!resuming}

When a chapter generation is interrupted partway through:

1. Save the current state (section drafts, citations collected, revisions made)
2. Create a checkpoint document: section completed, sections in progress, sections not yet started
3. In the next session, load the checkpoint document and the partial chapter
4. Use the checkpoint to quickly resume without re-reading full context

*** AI Agent Dos for Chapter Generation
\index{AI agents!chapter generation!dos}

1. Use the /rewritechapter skill, which is designed for this workflow, rather than attempting chapter generation without its structure.
2. Ask the AI to maintain a per-chapter checklist of requirements from the chapter brief and verify each is met before finalizing.
3. Have the AI generate a context summary at the end of each session for use in the next session.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Chapter Generation
\index{AI agents!chapter generation!do-nots}

1. Do not attempt to draft multiple chapters in a single session. Context constraints and review quality degrade.
2. Do not skip the chapter brief. It is the foundation for productive chapter generation.
3. Do not abandon a chapter mid-generation without saving a checkpoint.

*** AI Verification Chores for Chapter Generation
\index{AI agents!chapter generation!verification}

1. Verify that all requirements in the chapter brief are met before marking the chapter complete.
2. Check that all cross-references to other chapters are correctly labeled (will be resolved in final build).

** Cross-Chapter Consistency
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:cross-chapter-consistency
:END:
#+Latex:\index{cross-chapter consistency}
\index{consistency auditing}
\index{canonical source principle}
\index{AI agents!consistency management}

Consistency across chapters is maintained through shared resources and systematic auditing.

*** Prevention: Shared Resource Files
\index{shared resources}

- **Acronyms file**: Every acronym defined in one place; used consistently across chapters
- **Symbols file**: Mathematical notation standardized (does "p" mean probability or p-value?)
- **Glossary file**: Technical terms defined once; all chapters use the same definition
- **Shared bibliography**: All chapters cite from the same .bib file

*** Detection: Cross-Chapter Audit
\index{cross-chapter audit}

1. Ask the AI to extract all unique acronyms from each chapter and check them against the shared acronyms file
2. Have the AI scan for definition inconsistencies: "machine learning is..." in Chapter 2 vs. "machine learning refers to..." in Chapter 5
3. Use the AI to check for symbol redefinition: if Chapter 3 defines $\mu$ as mean, verify Chapter 4 uses it the same way
4. Ask the AI to identify terminology drift: if "supervised learning" is used in some chapters but "directed learning" in others

*** Resolution: Canonical Source Principle
\index{canonical source principle}

When inconsistency is detected, designate one chapter as the canonical source for that concept:

- The canonical chapter has the authoritative definition
- Other chapters reference the canonical chapter or defer to the glossary
- Update all chapters to use the canonical definition or terminology

*** AI Agent Dos for Cross-Chapter Consistency
\index{AI agents!consistency!dos}

1. Ask the AI to check each new chapter against the shared resources and flag any deviations.
2. Have the AI identify where definitions differ across chapters and suggest the canonical version.
3. Use the AI to perform a full cross-chapter audit before finalizing the book.
4. Ask the AI to generate a consistency report with sections: acronym consistency, symbol consistency, terminology consistency.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Cross-Chapter Consistency
\index{AI agents!consistency!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that consistency will happen naturally. Auditing is mandatory.
2. Do not allow chapters to "reinvent" shared concepts. Use the shared resources.

*** AI Verification Chores for Cross-Chapter Consistency
\index{AI agents!consistency!verification}

1. Review the consistency report and resolve all flagged issues before book publication.
2. Spot-check 10 acronyms across all chapters to verify consistency.

** Collaborative Labor Division Patterns
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:labor-division-patterns
:END:
#+Latex:\index{labor division}
\index{human-AI collaboration}
\index{authorial voice!preservation}
\index{AI agents!labor division}

Different chapters may be suited to different labor division patterns depending on their content and your expertise.

*** Pattern A: Traditional (Human Outline → AI Draft → Human Revise)
\index{pattern A!traditional}

- **Best for**: Standard exposition, literature-heavy chapters, areas where you want strong AI input
- **Effort**: High human revision burden (30–50% of final text may be rewritten)
- **Quality**: Depends entirely on revision quality
- **Workflow**:
  1. Human drafts detailed outline and key citations
  2. AI drafts full sections from outline
  3. Human revises for accuracy, voice, and coherence
  4. 1–2 revision passes typically needed

*** Pattern B: Hybrid (Human Framing → AI Technical → Human Synthesis)
\index{pattern B!hybrid}

- **Best for**: Mixed prose and technical sections, where you want to preserve your voice in framing
- **Effort**: Moderate (outline and framing already prepared by human)
- **Quality**: Higher (human voice is preserved in structure)
- **Workflow**:
  1. Human writes introduction/framing for section
  2. AI drafts technical exposition or analysis
  3. Human writes conclusion/synthesis
  4. Minimal revision needed if brief was clear

*** Pattern C: Artifact-Focused (Human Prose → AI Artifacts → Integration)
\index{pattern C!artifact-focused}

- **Best for**: Figure/table-heavy chapters, chapters with extensive code or data
- **Effort**: Low human effort (prose is human-written)
- **Quality**: Very high (prose is entirely human; artifacts are AI-assisted)
- **Workflow**:
  1. Human writes all prose sections
  2. AI generates figures, tables, code, analyses
  3. Human integrates artifacts into prose
  4. Verification of artifacts (see Phase 7)

*** AI Agent Dos for Labor Division
\index{AI agents!labor division!dos}

1. Ask the AI to assess the current chapter against the three patterns and suggest which fits best.
2. Have the AI clarify the boundary between human and AI contributions for the chosen pattern.
3. Use the AI for the assigned role in the chosen pattern; do not diverge mid-chapter.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Labor Division
\index{AI agents!labor division!do-nots}

1. Do not mix patterns within a single chapter. Pick one and stick with it.
2. Do not let labor division blur responsibility. You remain the author and must review thoroughly.

*** AI Verification Chores for Labor Division
\index{AI agents!labor division!verification}

1. After completion, verify that the authorial voice is consistent (check Pattern B and C especially).
2. Ensure that the prose-to-artifact ratio is appropriate for the chapter type.

** Six Quality Gate Criteria
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:quality-gates
:END:
#+Latex:\index{quality gates}
\index{quality assurance!book chapters}
\index{AI agents!quality verification}

Before advancing to the next chapter, verify that the current chapter meets all six criteria.

*** Criterion 1: Completeness
\index{quality gate!completeness}

- All sections from the chapter brief are present
- No placeholder text (TK, TODO, FIXME, [NEEDS CITATION])
- All promised figures and tables are included
- All cited sources are in the bibliography

*** Criterion 2: Accuracy
\index{quality gate!accuracy}

- All claims are verified against sources (see Phase 7 verification)
- All citations resolve and are correct
- All numerical values (statistics, measurements) are verified
- No hallucinated references
- Facts about historical events, people, dates are accurate

*** Criterion 3: Consistency
\index{quality gate!consistency}

- Terminology is consistent with shared glossary
- Acronyms are consistent with shared acronyms file
- Notation is consistent with shared symbols file
- Voice and tone are consistent within chapter and with other chapters
- Tense is consistent (past for history, present for timeless statements)

*** Criterion 4: Style
\index{quality gate!style}

- Scientific writing conventions are followed (active voice, precision, clarity)
- Prose matches the book's style guide (field-specific conventions)
- No contractions (do not, cannot, will not, etc.)
- "Because" is used for causation; "since" for temporal contexts
- Hedging language is appropriate (does not over-claim)

*** Criterion 5: Citation Quality
\index{quality gate!citation quality}

- All citations are formatted consistently per bibliography style
- All references are in the .bib file
- No formatting errors in BibTeX entries (special characters, braces, etc.)
- DOIs are present where available
- Author names are correctly capitalized

*** Criterion 6: Readability
\index{quality gate!readability}

- Flesch-Kincaid grade level is appropriate for audience
- Paragraphs have topic sentences and clear conclusions
- Transitions between sections are explicit
- Complex sentences are broken into simpler units where possible
- No orphaned paragraphs or isolated sections

*** AI Agent Dos for Quality Gates
\index{AI agents!quality gates!dos}

1. Ask the AI to run all six criteria checks on the completed chapter and generate a report.
2. Have the AI flag any criterion that is not fully met and suggest specific revisions.
3. Use the AI to verify that revisions address all flagged items.
4. Ask the AI to re-run the checks and confirm all criteria are met before marking chapter complete.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Quality Gates
\index{AI agents!quality gates!do-nots}

1. Do not skip quality gates to save time. Catching errors early prevents larger rework later.
2. Do not advance to the next chapter if any criterion is unmet. It creates cascading problems.

*** AI Verification Chores for Quality Gates
\index{AI agents!quality gates!verification}

1. Manually verify at least one criterion (e.g., completeness) by reading the chapter.
2. Spot-check the AI's assessment of other criteria.

** Project Timeline and Sequencing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:project-timeline
:END:
#+Latex:\index{project timeline}
\index{project scheduling}
\index{AI agents!project planning}

Different book projects require different sequencing strategies based on scope, complexity, and constraints.

*** Fast-Track Strategy
\index{fast-track sequencing}

- **Approach**: Draft all chapters, then do one consolidated revision pass
- **Timeline**: Faster initial drafting, but more rework later
- **Best for**: Books with tight deadlines, experienced authors, projects with clear structure
- **Effort**: Front-loaded on AI drafting, back-loaded on human revision

*** Careful Strategy
\index{careful sequencing}

- **Approach**: Review and revise each chapter before moving to the next
- **Timeline**: Slower but fewer surprises; catch issues early
- **Best for**: Complex books, first-time authors, books with high stakes (monographs, dissertations)
- **Effort**: Evenly distributed across drafting and revision

*** Iterative Strategy
\index{iterative sequencing}

- **Approach**: Waves of drafting (all chapters), then waves of revision (all chapters), repeating 2–3 times
- **Timeline**: Moderate duration; high quality
- **Best for**: Books addressing emerging topics, collaborative books, books requiring extensive fact-checking
- **Effort**: Multiple passes, but each pass is focused on specific issues (first pass: completeness; second pass: accuracy; third pass: style)

*** Completion Criteria
\index{project completion}

A book is complete when:

1. All chapters meet the six quality gate criteria
2. Cross-chapter consistency audit passes (Phase 10, Section "Cross-Chapter Consistency")
3. Front and back matter (title, preface, table of contents, index, bibliography) are complete
4. Full book compiles without errors or warnings
5. Entire book is proofread (proof review, Phase 9)

*** AI Agent Dos for Project Timeline
\index{AI agents!timeline!dos}

1. Ask the AI to estimate the timeline for each chapter based on your labor division pattern and chapter complexity.
2. Have the AI calculate an overall project timeline based on sequencing strategy.
3. Use the AI to create a Gantt chart or timeline showing chapter dependencies and estimated completion dates.
4. Ask the AI to flag any risks or bottlenecks in the timeline.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Project Timeline
\index{AI agents!timeline!do-nots}

1. Do not underestimate revision time. Plan for 30–50% additional time for revision beyond drafting.
2. Do not assume chapters can be drafted in parallel if they have dependencies. Sequence accordingly.

*** AI Verification Chores for Project Timeline
\index{AI agents!timeline!verification}

1. Track actual progress against the timeline and adjust as needed.
2. After project completion, analyze what factors caused timeline deviations.

** Copyright and Intellectual Property
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:copyright-ip
:END:
#+Latex:\index{copyright!AI-generated work}
\index{intellectual property}
\index{U.S. Copyright Office}
\index{sufficient human contribution}
\index{AI agents!copyright issues}

The question of whether AI-generated work is copyrightable has important implications for licensing and author rights.

*** U.S. Copyright Office Guidance
\index{Copyright Office guidance}

The U.S. Copyright Office has ruled that works lacking "sufficient human contribution" are not copyrightable.
For AI-assisted works:

- **Sufficient human contribution**: Extensive human decisions (outline selection, evidence curation, revision strategy) satisfy the requirement
- **Insufficient contribution**: Text passed through AI with no other human input likely does not meet the threshold
- **Practical implication**: AI-assisted academic writing (with human review and revision) should be copyrightable

*** Practical Implications
\index{copyright implications}

- **Licensing**: Licensors (journals, funders) can confidently license AI-assisted work
- **Open access mandates**: AI-assisted work qualifies for CC-BY and other open licenses
- **Author rights**: Authors retain copyright (or assign to journal) of AI-assisted work

*** AI Agent Dos for Copyright Compliance
\index{AI agents!copyright compliance!dos}

1. Ask the AI to review your contribution to the AI-assisted work (outline, evidence selection, revision) and assess whether it meets the "sufficient human contribution" threshold.
2. Have the AI draft a copyright statement for the work.
3. Use the AI to verify that the chosen license (CC-BY, CC0, etc.) is appropriate for AI-assisted work.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Copyright Compliance
\index{AI agents!copyright compliance!do-nots}

1. Do not submit AI-assisted work without being confident it meets the sufficient human contribution threshold. When in doubt, increase revision effort.
2. Do not assume that any license is appropriate for AI-assisted work without checking legal guidance.

*** AI Verification Chores for Copyright Compliance

** The Risk of Homogenization
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:homogenization-risk
:END:
#+Latex:\index{homogenization risk}
\index{scientific voice}
\index{distinctive perspective}
\index{convergence problem}
\index{AI agents!authorial voice}

If all papers in a field are drafted by the same large language model, using the same style rules and prompts, a subtle convergence occurs:
all prose becomes more similar, all arguments follow the same patterns, all papers sound alike.
This loss of distinctive perspective can impoverish science.

*** Mechanisms of Homogenization
\index{homogenization!mechanisms}

- **Same models**: When all AI assistance uses Claude, GPT, or the same smaller model, underlying patterns leak into output
- **Same prompts**: Identical or near-identical prompts across researchers amplify similarity
- **Same style rules**: When all authors follow the same scientific writing rules (active voice, precision emphasis), diversity decreases
- **Reduced revision**: Less human revision means less opportunity for individual voice to reshape prose

*** Impact on Science
\index{homogenization!impact}

- **Loss of perspective**: Science progresses when researchers challenge dominant interpretations. Homogenized prose dampens such challenges.
- **Subtle biases**: All models have training biases. Homogenized output means all papers share the same biases.
- **Reduced creativity**: Distinctive voice often comes from creative prose and novel argument structures. These are first to disappear.

*** Countermeasures
\index{homogenization!countermeasures}

1. **Preserve authorial voice**: Use chapter briefs to encode your perspective; revise heavily for interpretive slant
2. **Use AI for support, not replacement**: Do not draft entire sections. Use AI for polish, evidence location, outline generation.
3. **Revise with purpose**: Set a revision goal: "This section should emphasize uncertainty" or "This paragraph should be provocative." Then revise the AI draft toward that goal.
4. **Editorial vigilance**: When reviewing papers, challenge formulaic language. Reward distinctive voice.

*** AI Agent Dos for Preserving Voice
\index{AI agents!voice preservation!dos}

1. Ask the AI to identify sections that sound generic or formulaic and suggest how to infuse personality.
2. Have the AI compare your current chapter voice against your prior publications to check for drift.
3. Use the AI to generate multiple stylistic variants of a passage and choose the most distinctively yours.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Preserving Voice
\index{AI agents!voice preservation!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that AI output captures your voice. It is an approximation; heavy revision is needed.
2. Do not use the same prompts for every chapter. Vary the framing and emphasis.

*** AI Verification Chores for Voice Preservation
\index{AI agents!voice preservation!verification}

1. Read a page from your current manuscript and a page from your prior publication side-by-side. Is the voice recognizably yours?
2. Ask a colleague whether they can distinguish your writing from that of peers in the field.

** Bias Propagation in AI-Assisted Literature Reviews
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:bias-propagation
:END:
#+Latex:\index{bias!literature reviews}
\index{language bias}
\index{citation bias}
\index{institutional bias}
\index{Matthew effect}
\index{AI agents!bias mitigation}

When AI assists with literature searches and synthesis, it can inadvertently amplify existing biases in the literature.

*** Language Bias
\index{bias!language}

**Problem**: AI training data is dominated by English-language publications.
Spanish, Chinese, Portuguese, French, and other language literature is under-represented.
Result: AI-assisted literature reviews are English-centric.

**Mitigation**:
- Supplement AI search with manual searches in non-English databases (CNKI for Chinese, SciELO for Latin America)
- Explicitly include searches in 2–3 non-English databases
- Note in the methods that language bias was acknowledged and partially mitigated
- Acknowledge limitations of English-language bias in discussion

*** Citation Bias (Matthew Effect)
\index{bias!citation!Matthew effect}

**Problem**: AI (and humans) preferentially cite already highly-cited papers.
This creates a feedback loop: highly-cited papers are recommended more, become more cited.
Under-cited papers, even if important, are less likely to be found.

**Mitigation**:
- Follow backward citations: when you find a key paper, check its references
- Search for papers that cite the most important work, then search their references
- Examine citation networks (e.g., via Citespace or VOSviewer) to find important papers not yet cited
- Deliberately include papers from lower-citation tiers

*** Institutional Bias
\index{bias!institutional}

**Problem**: Well-resourced institutions (Ivy League, major research universities, wealthy countries) are over-represented.
Global South institutions are under-represented.

**Mitigation**:
- Include diversity metrics in literature searches: geographic diversity, institutional type diversity
- Deliberately search for papers from under-resourced regions
- Note in methods any geographic or institutional biases

*** AI Agent Dos for Bias Mitigation
\index{AI agents!bias mitigation!dos}

1. Ask the AI to assess the literature search for language, citation, and institutional bias.
2. Have the AI identify papers from non-English databases that address the same topics.
3. Use the AI to generate a bias mitigation plan for the literature section.
4. Ask the AI to flag any high-citation papers that may be dominating the literature summary unnaturally.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Bias Mitigation
\index{AI agents!bias mitigation!do-nots}

1. Do not assume that AI will naturally discover under-represented literature. Manual search is necessary.
2. Do not include papers just to "balance" citations. Include them because they are important.

*** AI Verification Chores for Bias Mitigation
\index{AI agents!bias mitigation!verification}

1. Scan the bibliography for geographic and language diversity.
2. Verify that at least 10% of citations are from lower-citation-tier papers.

** AI in Peer Review
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:ai-peer-review
:END:
#+Latex:\index{peer review!AI use}
\index{reviewer disclosure}
\index{symmetry principle}
\index{AI agents!reviewer assistance}

As authors use AI to draft responses to reviewer comments, a question arises: should reviewers also be allowed (or required) to disclose AI use?

*** Author Use: Drafting Reviewer Responses
\index{reviewer response!AI assistance}

**Current practice**: Authors increasingly use AI to draft responses to reviewer comments.
**Rationale**: This is a natural extension of help-seeking (asking colleagues, editors for advice).
**Precedent**: Authors use statistical consultants, writing coaches, professional editors; reviewer response assistance is similar.

*** Reviewer Use: More Contentious
\index{reviewer use!concerns}

**Issues**:
- Confidentiality: AI systems may be cloud-based; reviewer confidentiality could be breached
- Quality: AI-drafted reviews may lack domain expertise
- Disclosure asymmetry: Author discloses; reviewer does not, creating imbalance

*** The Symmetry Principle
\index{symmetry principle}

**Proposal**: If authors must disclose AI use, reviewers should too.
**Benefits**: Transparency, fairness, enables editors to assess review quality
**Challenges**: Reviewer disclosure overhead, potential for reviewer backlash

*** Current State and Trajectory
\index{peer review trajectory}

- Some journals now require reviewer disclosure of AI use
- Others are moving in that direction
- No universal standard yet, but symmetry is increasingly adopted

*** AI Agent Dos for Reviewer Responses
\index{AI agents!reviewer response!dos}

1. Use AI to draft reviewer response letters (this is acceptable and increasingly normal)
2. Disclose AI use in the response letter or cover letter
3. Ask the AI to help organize responses but ensure substantive content (addressing actual criticisms) is yours

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Reviewer Responses
\index{AI agents!reviewer response!do-nots}

1. Do not use AI to fabricate evidence or make unfounded defenses. The AI output must be factually correct.
2. Do not let AI defensiveness overshadow valid criticisms. Some reviewer comments should change your manuscript.

*** AI Verification Chores for Reviewer Responses
\index{AI agents!reviewer response!verification}

1. Verify that every reviewer comment receives a substantive response (not evasion).
2. Have a colleague review the response letter for tone and appropriateness.

** Reproducibility and Transparency Requirements

Reproducibility in AI-assisted work means enabling others to understand what AI was used, how, and with what results.

*** What Should Be Shared
\index{artifact sharing!what to share}

1. **AI model name and version**: "Claude 3.5 Sonnet (October 2024)"
2. **Prompts and skills used**: Complete or representative prompts; code for custom skills
3. **Chapter briefs and grounding documents**: Specifications and context provided to AI
4. **Verification reports**: Results of verification (Phase 7) showing what was checked and what issues were found
5. **Revision logs**: Before-and-after of major revisions to show how AI output was modified
6. **Shared resource files**: Acronyms, symbols, glossary used across chapters

*** Sharing Mechanisms
\index{artifact sharing!mechanisms}

1. **Supplementary materials**: Verification reports, chapter briefs, shared resources
2. **GitHub/GitLab**: Full .claude/ directory with all prompts, skills, logs, permanent Zenodo DOI
3. **Registered reports**: For methodologically significant AI use, register the approach before execution
4. **Preprint materials**: arXiv has supplementary material support

*** Transparency Checklist
\index{transparency checklist}

- [ ] Model name and version disclosed
- [ ] Prompts and skills disclosed (or summary)
- [ ] Chapter briefs provided (if book project)
- [ ] Verification reports included
- [ ] Shared resource files documented
- [ ] Revision strategy explained
- [ ] Limitations of AI use acknowledged

*** AI Agent Dos for Transparency
\index{AI agents!transparency!dos}

1. Ask the AI to help compile all artifacts into a shareable format (GitHub repo, supplementary materials package).
2. Have the AI generate a README documenting all shared artifacts and how to use them.
3. Use the AI to create a transparency statement summarizing AI use for the manuscript.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Transparency
\index{AI agents!transparency!do-nots}

1. Do not withhold artifacts claiming confidentiality. Science depends on transparency.
2. Do not share prompts that compromise your intellectual approach, but do share enough for reproducibility.

*** AI Verification Chores for Transparency
\index{AI agents!transparency!verification}

1. Verify that all shared artifacts are complete and functional.
2. Test that someone using only the shared artifacts could roughly replicate your work.

** Four Core Principles for Responsible AI-Assisted Scholarship
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: subsec:core-principles
:END:
#+Latex:\index{core principles}
\index{responsible AI}

As you navigate the future of AI-assisted scholarship, keep four principles central:

*** 1. The Human Is the Author
\index{core principle!human author}

- You decide what to say
- You choose what evidence to present
- You determine the interpretation
- AI generates text; you accept or revise
- You take responsibility for every word

*** 2. Verification Is Non-Negotiable
\index{core principle!verification}

- Every claim must be checked against source
- The cost of verification is far less than the cost of retraction
- Verification is not optional or deferrable
- Systematic verification (Phase 7) is part of the writing process

*** 3. Transparency Is an Obligation
\index{core principle!transparency}

- Disclose AI use per journal policy
- Share artifacts (prompts, briefs, verification reports) to enable reproducibility
- Document your workflow for others to learn from
- Acknowledge limitations of AI use

*** 4. The Tool Serves the Craft
\index{core principle!tool serves craft}

- AI amplifies your ability to write clearly and verify thoroughly
- It does not replace your judgment or expertise
- A power tool amplifies a carpenter's ability; it does not replace carpentry
- Use AI to focus your effort on what you do best (thinking, synthesizing, judging)

** AI-Enhanced Daily Protocol (Expanded)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sub:ai-daily-protocol-expanded
:END:
#+Latex:\index{daily protocol!expanded}
\index{session structure}
\index{context management}

The daily protocol structures each writing session to maximize productivity and maintain momentum.

*** Morning Routine (10 minutes)
\index{daily protocol!morning}

1. Open the writing log (text file or Markdown document).
2. Ask the AI to summarize the timeline, recent daily entries, next action item, and to-do list from the log.
3. Write the session goal(s) in the log as a means of engaging mentally with the work.
4. Review the shared resource files (acronyms, symbols, glossary) to refresh your memory of key terms.

*** During Session (core work)
\index{daily protocol!during session}

1. Work on the assigned writing task (section drafting, revision, verification).
2. Use the AI to convert rough notes into polished prose.
3. Use the AI for verification: check citations, validate claims, scan for logical issues.
4. Document any changes to shared resources (new acronyms, revised definitions).
5. Save changes frequently and commit to git at logical breakpoints.

*** Evening Routine (15 minutes)
\index{daily protocol!evening}

1. Ask the AI to generate the Hemingway bridge: next actions, current challenges, fleeting details, intention for tomorrow.
2. Move finished items to the accomplishment list; move unfinished items to the to-do list.
3. Ask the AI to update the wordcount.txt file with today's count and cumulative progress.
4. Update the Writing Progress Notebook (table with date, section, words written, notes).
5. Ask the AI to identify any new knowledge base entries (permanent notes, literature notes) generated during the session.
6. Create a git commit with today's work: =git commit -m "Draft [section name]: [brief description]"=

*** Context Management for Long Projects
\index{context management!long projects}

As projects extend beyond a few sessions:

1. **Story So Far document**: After 3–4 sessions, ask the AI to create a summary of what has been completed, decisions made, and current status. Save this in the project directory.
2. **Checkpoint markers**: Every 5 sessions, save a snapshot of the project (git tag) with a date and description.
3. **Progress dashboard**: Maintain a simple table (chapter, draft %, revision %, verification %) for visibility.
4. **Context reset**: When approaching token limits or when switching projects, use the Story So Far document to onboard the AI into the next session.

*** AI Agent Dos for Daily Protocol
\index{AI agents!daily protocol!dos}

1. Ask the AI to help enforce the daily protocol structure (reminders for morning/evening routines).
2. Have the AI maintain the wordcount and progress tracking automatically.
3. Use the AI to generate the Hemingway bridge consistently; it becomes a valuable accountability mechanism.

*** AI Agent Do-Nots for Daily Protocol
\index{AI agents!daily protocol!do-nots}

1. Do not skip the evening routine to save time. It is essential for continuity.
2. Do not let the AI maintain the writing log without your input. The log must capture your thinking, not just AI suggestions.

*** AI Verification Chores for Daily Protocol
\index{AI agents!daily protocol!verification}

1. Weekly, review the log to verify that the AI's summaries are accurate and helpful.
2. Monthly, assess productivity metrics (words written, sections completed, verification issues found) to identify patterns.
